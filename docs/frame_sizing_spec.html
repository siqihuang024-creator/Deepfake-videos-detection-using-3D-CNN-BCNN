CelebDFv3 and DFD Frame Sizing

Two experiments, run against each other to see which preprocessing gives the better result. Sizes chosen from a resolution audit of all 57,517 videos in the two corpora.

COMMONApplies to both experiments

The whole frame is kept — no cropping, no face detection. Each frame is scaled to fit the target size with its aspect ratio preserved, then padded on the short side with replicated edge pixels.

Resize
Factor min(Wₜ/W, Hₜ/H); area interpolation when reducing, bilinear when enlarging
Padding
Replicated edge pixels, split evenly between both sides
Normalisation
ImageNet channel statistics
Temporal
8 frames per clip (3D model); unchanged by either experiment
Splits
Identity-disjoint; unchanged by either experiment

EXPERIMENT 1A separate input size per dataset

Each corpus is trained, validated and tested at the size that suits it. In the two cross-dataset settings the test corpus is converted to whatever size the model was trained at — the transformation it undergoes is the matching column of Table 1.

Choice of sizes

CelebDFv3 → 512×512. The corpus holds 147 distinct resolutions with a median long side of 512 and a median aspect ratio of 1.00; 256×256 accounts for 42.6% of it and 512×512 for a further 15.9%.

DFD → 1280×720. Every DFD video is 1920×1080. Native size is not affordable — the first convolution over a 1920×1080 input produces roughly 43× the activations of a 224×224 one — so it is reduced by 0.67 onto the nearest standard 16:9 size, which needs no padding.

EXPERIMENT 2One input size for both datasets

Both corpora are mapped to 896×504, and the same transformation is used for training, validation and test in all four settings. Nothing is converted at test time.

Why unify

A convolutional filter is tied to a pixel scale, so two things have to agree between training and test: how many pixels the structures being detected occupy, and how much of the canvas the subject fills. In experiment 1 the cross-dataset settings break both, and break them only at test time — in setting 3 DFD arrives at ×0.27 against the ×0.60 the model trained on, and in setting 4 CelebDFv3 arrives enlarged by ×1.44 or more. A shared size removes the conversion, so a cross-dataset score reflects the difference between the corpora rather than a transformation applied to one of them at the last moment.

Why 896×504. On the standard 16:9 ladder (width 128k, height 72k) it is the first rung at which CelebDFv3 is no longer reduced below its native resolution: ×1.01 for the real videos and ×1.05 for the wide fakes, so nothing is lost to resampling. One rung down (768×432) is ×0.86, discarding 14% of the linear detail; one rung up (1024×576) is pure interpolation for 31% more compute. The 16:9 shape follows the real videos (median aspect 1.79) and all of DFD (1.78).

The shaded cells in Table 1 show one property that resizing cannot remove: in CelebDFv3 aspect ratio is correlated with the label — 93.9% of its real videos are wide and 65.1% of its fakes are square or near-square (59.5% exactly square) — so under any canvas the padded fraction itself carries class information, only in opposite directions at 512×512 and at 896×504.
