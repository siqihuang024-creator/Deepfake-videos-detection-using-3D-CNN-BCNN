Frame Processing for CelebDFv3 and DFD

Published practice on these two corpora, measurements on the proposed 892×500 centre crop, and a revised proposal. CelebDFv3 is Celeb-DF++.

01Published practice

Work on these corpora follows one pipeline: detect the face, crop it with a 1.3 margin, resize to a fixed square. None uses whole frames.

The third row governs comparability: the benchmark's own published baselines were produced under those defaults — GF-eval 71.7% frame-AUC and 72.1% video-AUC, GFD-eval 69.4% and 73.7%. Our results can only be placed beside those figures if the preprocessing matches.

02The 892×500 centre crop, measured

892×500 is the median of the real videos; the fakes and the second corpus differ.

Most of CelebDFv3 cannot supply it. The crop needs a source at least 892 wide and 500 tall. Only 6.5% of CelebDFv3 qualifies (3,533 of 54,086): 23,059 videos are 256×256 and 8,579 are 512×512, and even the wide fakes at 856×478 fall short on both axes. The largest crop every CelebDFv3 video can supply is 256×208. DFD qualifies at 100%.

In DFD the face is not at the centre. Across 40 sampled videos, a face was found in 39; the median box is 213×213 px, 11% of frame width, with its centre a median 251 px above the frame centre. A centred 892×500 crop holds the face fully in 1 of 39 videos and partially in 37 — heads sit in the upper third of a framed shot, so a 500-px band through the middle cuts at the jaw.

03Result of the whole-frame runs

The detector is trained in two stages. Stage A pre-trains the feature extractor: the 3D-CNN is given an ordinary deterministic classification head and trained on real/fake labels; the head is then discarded and only the extractor weights are kept. Stage B freezes that extractor, attaches the Bayesian fully-connected head, and trains one-class on real videos only. What follows is Stage A.

Frames were letterboxed: scaled by min(Wₜ/W, Hₜ/H) to fit inside the canvas, with the short side made up by edge-pixel replication (cv2.BORDER_REPLICATE) split evenly on both sides. Two extractors, one per corpus, 60 epochs each at 896×504.

As a control we built a ranker that reads only the share of the canvas occupied by that replicated border, ignoring image content entirely. Neither network exceeded it.

Possible explanations differ by corpus, and both need further experiments to confirm.

  1. CelebDFv3. Real videos are 93.9% wide and fakes 65.1% square, so border share is correlated with the label; 13 of the 22 forgery methods emit no wide video at all. The network may be relying substantially on that correlation rather than on manipulation traces.
  2. DFD. Every video is 1920×1080, so no such correlation exists, and the network learned little that transfers — training loss settled at 0.673 against ln 2 = 0.693. One possible cause is the small share of the input the face occupies: with a 213 px face box and a ×0.47 scale, roughly 2.2% of the input pixels, against about 6% for CelebDFv3.

04Revised proposal: face crop

Adopt the published pipeline: detect the face, enlarge the box ×1.3, resize to 256×256. The 1.3 margin was established by FaceForensics++ (Rössler et al., ICCV 2019), fixed as the default by DeepfakeBench (Yan et al., NeurIPS 2023 Datasets & Benchmarks), and used to produce the Celeb-DF++ baselines in Table 1. Adopting it is what makes our results comparable to mainstream work.

It also improves the resampling situation. Frame size differs across the corpora by a factor of 3.75; face size barely differs at all.

Two further consequences. There is no padding, so the border-share correlation disappears and all 22 forgery methods stay usable — against 9 that survive a shape-controlled subset of the whole-frame data. And the face occupies 59% of the input rather than 2.2–6%. 256×256 is also the native size of CelebDFv3's own square fakes.

05Costs

  1. Detection failure is silent. A wrong box feeds a wrong crop and raises no error — so we log per-video detection rate and box stability, and withhold what falls below threshold.
  2. The crop discards background, where blending boundaries and background inconsistency also live. The 1.3 margin keeps some of it; this is a trade, not a free gain.
  3. Cropping can introduce its own shortcut if the detector behaves differently on fakes — so we check the per-class distribution of box size, as we did for border share.

The 1.3 margin is a convention, not an optimum; we adopt it for comparability and can ablate it later against that baseline. For the detector we start with DLIB to match the protocol, measure detection rate first, and substitute a stronger detector with a noted deviation if it fails on DFD's 1080p scenes, as the Haar cascade did.

06Next steps

  1. Cache face boxes for both corpora in one offline pass, with the detection-rate report.
  2. Re-score the two existing whole-frame extractors on a shape-matched subset, so that result stands as a measured ablation.
  3. Re-run Stage A at face crop ×1.3 → 256×256, one extractor per corpus.
  4. Stage B, then the 2×2 matrix, reported alongside the baselines in Section 01.
